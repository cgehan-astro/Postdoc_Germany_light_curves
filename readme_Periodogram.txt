### Project: Periodogram.py

I completed this project during my postdoc in Göttingen, Germany (2021 - 2024).


### Project overview

This project aims at building a Lomb-Scargle periodogram from a light curve, i.e. flux time-serie, where the flux comes from stars. The emergence of magnetic field lines at the stellar surface lead to the formation of active regions either darker (spots) or brighter (faculae) than the surrounding surface; those active regions are co-rotating with the surface of stars and their transit in front of the visible stellar disk results in a periodic variation of the flux.


### Dataset

The dataset is composed of synthetic light curves, stored in ./Lightcurves_Aspot_15_MSH/* as .txt files. The light curves include flux variations caused by active regions co-rotating with the surface of stars, i.e. generated by internal magnetic fields: the files LC_fac_*.txt include the contribution to the flux of regions brighter than the surrounding stellar surface (i.e. faculae), the files LC_spot_*.txt include the contribution to the flux of regions darker than the surrounding stellar surface (i.e. spots), and the files LC_*.txt include the final light curves (i.e. the contributions to the flux of both faculae and spots). The files Parameters_*.txt contain physical parameters used to generate the synthetic light curves: if differential rotation between the equator and the poles of the star is implemented or not, the value of the period at which the star rotates on itself, a parameter scaling the level of magnetic activity caused by internal magnetic fields to use, an indicator of the level of magnetic activity, a parameter quantifying whether active regions appearing successively are nested or not, and the number of the realisation used to generate the ight curve (the longitude and time at which the active regions successively appear are random).


### Methodology

The raw light curves generated by the program Lightcurve_with_activity_one_spot.py are noiseless: white noise representative of the noise level expected from the PLATO space mission is added from the file Example_PLATO_LC_PSLS_no_systematics_no_activity_no_granulation_P1_NSR_50_ppm.dat. The light curves are then zero-padded in order to increase the future resolution in the frequency domain. The Lomb-Scargle periodogram is computed by taking the Fourier transform of the light curve because our synthetic data points are evenly spaced in time; the power is finally obtained by taking the squared absolute value of the Fourier transform.


### Results

The plots Light_curve_1_spot_*_rotation_periods.pdf show two synthetic light curves, i.e. flux time-series, which have been generated for a synthetic star which rotation axis is inclined by 60 degrees from the line-of-sight, and that has one active region co-rotating with its surface and located at the equator of the star (latitude of 0 degree), under the form of a dark spot. The two light curves are generated over a time span of 730 days, i.e. 2 years. The only difference between the two light curves is the lifetime of the spot: it lives for 3 rotation periods of the star in the plot Light_curve_1_spot_3_rotation_periods.pdf while it lives for 6 rotation periods of the star in the plot Light_curve_1_spot_6_rotation_periods.pdf, with an linear temporal decay of its contribution to the light curve. The noiseless spot contribution to the light curve is shown in orange; since the spot is darker than the surounding surface of the star, it generates a decrease in the flux when it transit in front of the visible stellar disk. The total light curve including white noise representative of the PLATO space mission is shown in blue. The plot Lomb_Scargle_periodogram.pdf shows the Lomb-Scargle periodograms of the two light curves plotted in Light_curve_1_spot_*_rotation_periods.pdf, in blue for the light curve where the spot lives for 3 rotation periods of the star and in red for the light curve where the spot lives for 6 rotation periods of the star. The location of the period associated with the rotation period of the star (Prot) is identified by vertical black dashed lines, while the location of the period associated with the second harmonic of the rotation period of the star (Prot/2) is identified by vertical black dotted lines. This plots highlights that the peaks associated to the rotation period and its second harmonic are significantly easier to detect for longer spot lifetimes, as the power increases and the width of the peaks decreases with increasing spot lifetime.
